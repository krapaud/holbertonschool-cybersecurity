NOVATECH SOLUTIONS
IDENTITY SECURITY POLICY

Document owner: Chief Information Security Officer (CISO)
Applies to: Employees, contractors, administrators, service accounts, and
           systems managed by NovaTech Solutions
Review frequency: At least annually and after a material security incident

1. Purpose and scope
--------------------
This policy establishes minimum requirements for creating, authenticating,
managing, and removing identities that access NovaTech systems and data.
System owners must implement these controls and retain evidence of exceptions
and reviews. Access must be assigned to named identities and follow least
privilege.

2. Password policy
------------------
- Passwords must be at least 12 characters long and contain at least one
  uppercase letter, one lowercase letter, one digit, and one special character.
- A password must not contain the account username and must differ from the
  previous password by at least three characters.
- The last five passwords must not be reused.
- Passwords expire after 90 days. Users must wait at least one day between
  changes and receive a warning 14 days before expiration.
- Passwords must be unique to NovaTech accounts, kept confidential, and never
  stored in plaintext or shared. Suspected compromise requires an immediate
  reset and incident report.
- Password aging defaults apply to new accounts. Account owners and system
  administrators must ensure the same limits are applied to existing accounts.

3. Account lockout policy
-------------------------
- PAM must lock an account after five failed authentication attempts within a
  15-minute period.
- The lockout lasts 15 minutes. Administrators may reset a lock only after
  confirming the user's identity and checking for suspicious activity.
- Fail2ban must protect SSH by banning an IP address after five failures within
  ten minutes for one hour, where the service is deployed and supported.
- Repeated lockouts or unusual authentication patterns must be reviewed and
  escalated to the security team.

4. SSH access policy
--------------------
- SSH access is limited to authorized users with a documented business need.
- Public-key authentication using approved keys is required. ED25519 is the
  preferred key type. Private keys must be protected with a passphrase and
  owner-only permissions; public keys may be installed only after validation.
- Password-based SSH authentication is disabled. Root login over SSH is
  disabled. Administrators use named accounts and sudo for privileged actions.
- SSH configuration changes must be backed up, validated with sshd -t, and
  reloaded using the approved change process. Keep a separate recovery session
  or console available when changing remote access controls.
- Keys must be revoked promptly when access is no longer authorized or a key
  may have been exposed.

5. Multi-factor authentication requirements
--------------------------------------------
- SSH access to production and other designated sensitive systems requires
  both a valid public key and a time-based one-time password (TOTP).
- The SSH authentication sequence must require publickey followed by
  keyboard-interactive authentication backed by PAM and
  pam_google_authenticator.so.
- TOTP secrets must be stored in the user's protected home directory with
  owner-only permissions. The same TOTP code must not be reused. Apply rate
  limiting of no more than three attempts in 30 seconds and a token window of
  three time steps.
- Users must securely store emergency scratch codes offline. They are
  single-use credentials and must not be transmitted or recorded in tickets.
- New users must enroll and verify TOTP before receiving access to systems that
  require MFA. Lost or compromised factors must be revoked and replaced.
- MFA exceptions require documented approval, a named owner, a narrow scope,
  an expiry time, monitoring, and verified restoration. Password authentication
  must not be enabled as an emergency shortcut.

6. Account lifecycle
--------------------
Creation:
- A manager or system owner must approve each account and its business purpose.
- Provision a unique named account, verify the user's identity, assign only the
  groups and privileges needed for the role, and record the owner and review
  date. Shared human accounts are prohibited.
- Deliver initial access through an approved secure channel. Require the user
  to set a private password, deploy their own SSH public key where needed, and
  enroll required MFA before access is granted.

Modification:
- Managers must request role or privilege changes through the approved access
  process. The system owner approves the change and the administrator records
  what changed and when.
- Remove permissions that are no longer needed. Review privileged accounts,
  SSH keys, and MFA enrollment at least quarterly and after role changes.
- Service accounts must have a named owner, documented purpose, minimum
  permissions, and a defined credential rotation and review process. They must
  not be used for interactive human access unless explicitly approved.

Termination:
- On termination or contract end, disable interactive access promptly and no
  later than the end of the user's authorized period. Revoke SSH keys, active
  sessions, tokens, and other credentials; remove group and sudo privileges.
- The manager and system owner must identify data or services requiring
  transfer to a successor. Preserve records according to retention policy and
  document completion of access removal.

7. Emergency access procedures
------------------------------
Use the separate Identity and SSH MFA Emergency Access Procedure
(20-emergency_procedures.txt) for lost devices, scratch-code recovery,
temporary exceptions, or administrator recovery. Verify identity through an
independent channel, obtain approval, prefer console access or a separately
protected break-glass identity, retain SSH key authentication, set an expiry,
and restore MFA immediately after recovery. Keep root SSH login disabled.
Record approvals, operators, times, systems, actions, validation, and rollback
in the approved incident system. Never record TOTP seeds or scratch-code values.

8. Monitoring, exceptions, and compliance
-----------------------------------------
Authentication events, account changes, privileged actions, key changes, MFA
recovery, and exceptions must be logged and protected from unauthorized
alteration. The security team reviews relevant alerts and investigates
unusual activity. Exceptions require written CISO or delegated approval and
must be reviewed before expiry. Policy violations may result in access
suspension and further action under company procedures.
