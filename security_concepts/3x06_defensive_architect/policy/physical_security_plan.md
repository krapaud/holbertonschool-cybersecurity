# Physical Security Plan

## Objective and Scope

The goal of this plan is to protect Nexus Financial's people, offices,
workstations, servers and network equipment. The plan uses realistic measures
that can be applied quickly without buying biometric guards or expensive new
systems.

The main problems found during the walkthrough are uncontrolled building
access, an open server room, unlocked laptops, exposed passwords, shared
badges and active unused network ports.

## Immediate Actions: No Cost

These actions should be completed today using the existing staff and tools:

- Keep the server room door closed and remove the fire extinguisher used to
  hold it open.
- Assign one employee to control visitors until a permanent process is ready.
- Require every visitor to sign in and stay with an employee while inside.
- Stop anyone from entering the server room without approval.
- Erase all passwords from the whiteboard and report any exposed credential
  so it can be changed.
- Require employees to lock their MacBooks whenever they leave their desk.
- Collect the spare badges, count them and keep them in a controlled place.
- Label the existing badges and immediately disable badges that cannot be
  linked to a specific person.
- Disconnect or disable unused network ports if this can be done safely.
- Record these actions and keep the record for the final audit.

## Short-Term Actions: Low Cost

These actions should be completed during the next few weeks:

- Create a simple visitor procedure with sign-in, a temporary badge and an
  employee escort.
- Repair the biometric door or replace it with a normal lock that closes
  automatically.
- Create individual access badges instead of using one generic developer
  badge.
- Keep a list of active badges, their owners and their access areas.
- Review the list when someone joins, changes role or leaves the company.
- Buy a small lockable cabinet for spare badges and sensitive documents.
- Mark a clear delivery area so delivery workers do not enter the server room.
- Organize and label network cables so unauthorized connections are easier to
  detect.
- Put a clear desk and clear screen rule in the security policy.
- Use screen-lock settings on company laptops and check that they are active.

## Long-Term Actions

The long-term plan should improve both physical security and accountability:

- Install an access control system that records who enters the office and the
  server room.
- Add cameras at the entrance and near the server room, following applicable
  privacy rules.
- Use separate access zones for employees, visitors, the office and the
  server room.
- Add temperature, smoke and power monitoring for the server room.
- Replace the open coworking layout around critical equipment with a locked
  and restricted area.
- Move critical systems and databases to a professional data center or a
  secure cloud provider with physical security, redundancy and access logs.
- Deploy a Mobile Device Management (MDM) solution for company laptops. It
  should enforce encryption, screen locking, inventory and remote lock or
  wipe.
- Review physical access rights at least every quarter.
- Include physical access removal in the employee offboarding process.
- Test the visitor process and the response to lost badges regularly.
- Include physical security checks in the internal audit before the IPO.
- Schedule an independent professional security audit every year and before
  important business events such as the IPO.

## Training and the Delivery Guy TikTok Situation

Employees must understand that a delivery worker cannot enter the server room
or film near company equipment without authorization. The person should be
politely stopped and taken back to the delivery area by an employee.

The employee responsible for the visitor should inform the office manager and
the security contact. The incident should be documented with the date, time,
people involved and what was filmed. If company equipment or information was
visible, the company should request that the video is deleted and keep the
details as evidence.

Training should be repeated during onboarding and at least once a year. It
should teach employees to challenge unknown visitors politely, protect
confidential information, report lost badges and never leave a workstation
unlocked. Employees should not confront a person aggressively or try to take
their phone by force.

## Verification and Follow-up

The office manager should keep a checklist for the immediate and short-term
actions. The checklist should show the owner, the due date and the evidence
that the action was completed. The final audit should verify that the server
room is closed, spare badges are controlled, passwords are no longer exposed
and unused network ports are disabled.
